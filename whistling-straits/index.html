Strokes

How they are given

The difference between indexes, rounded, dropped on the lowest-index holes on that day’s card — all four of which are now on this page. Recalculated per course, because the tees change and so does the maths.

The Cup

In the event of a halve

Seven points, so somebody normally wins outright — but halved matches can still leave it three and a half apiece. There is no time for extra holes with flights to catch, so a level Cup is retained by the holder.

Local Knowledge

What the caddie will tell you on the first tee, minus the fee.

  1. Take one more club. The wind off Lake Michigan is heavier and steadier than it looks from the clubhouse. Into it, take two and swing easier.
  2. Everything is a bunker. On the Straits, the sand outside the ropes is still sand. Ask before you ground the club — it has cost better players than us.
  3. Walk it properly. No carts on the Straits or the Irish, and Friday is thirty-six of them. Broken-in shoes, two pairs of socks, and a real lunch in between.
  4. Take the line you are given. The caddies read break off the lake that is invisible from where you are standing. The man who overrules his caddie on Saturday will be hearing about it on Sunday.
  1. Play the front edge. Firm turf, low approaches, putter from off the green. This is not target golf, and the high soft one you have been practising will not be rewarded.
  2. Layers. August in the village and August on the eighth tee are two different months. A shell in the bag, every round, no exceptions.
  3. Sunday is a cart day. Meadow Valleys allows them. After three days on foot, take one and do not feel a thing about it.
  4. Look up on seventeen. Whatever the number ends up being, stand on that tee an extra moment. That is the one all four of you will still be describing in ten years.
