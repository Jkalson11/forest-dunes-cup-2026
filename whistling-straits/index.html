The Courses

Five Pete Dye designs within ten miles of one another. Two on the lake, three on the river.

The Straits

Whistling Straits · Pete Dye, 1998

Two miles of Lake Michigan shoreline rebuilt to look as though the glacier left it that way. Eight holes run along the water, the bunkering is uncountable by design, and the wind decides which golf course you get that morning.

Local Knowledge

What the caddie will tell you on the first tee, minus the fee.

  1. Take one more club. The wind off Lake Michigan is heavier and steadier than it looks from the clubhouse. Into it, take two and swing easier.
  2. Everything is a bunker. On the Straits, the sand outside the ropes is still sand. Ask before you ground the club — it has cost better players than us.
  3. Walk it properly. No carts on the Straits or the Irish, and Friday is thirty-six of them. Broken-in shoes, two pairs of socks, and a real lunch in between.
  4. Trust the caddie on the greens. They are reading break you cannot see from a hundred yards inland. Take the line and hit it.
  1. Play the front edge. Firm turf, low approaches, putter from off the green. This is not target golf and it does not reward flying it in.
  2. Layers. It can be twenty degrees cooler on the lake holes than in the village, even in August. A shell in the bag is never wasted.
  3. Sunday is a cart day. Meadow Valleys allows them. After three days on foot, take one and do not feel a thing about it.
  4. Look up on seventeen. Whatever the number ends up being, stand on that tee an extra moment. That is the one you will both still be describing in ten years.
